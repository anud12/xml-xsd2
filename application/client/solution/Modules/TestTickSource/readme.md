## Theme
Dieselpunk set in WW1 era of technology, where analog/mechanical technology is mass-produced,
and what now can be done with electricity, here its done by magic.
Everything is powered by majority of distilled oil (petrol/kerosine/diesel/fuel oil), rest is coal.
And other more efficient power sources aren't being investigated since it can be offset by magic use. 


Magic users are rare, and magic as a commodity itself is uncommon.

### Available historical technology 
#### Locomotion
- Tracked locomotion technology exists and could replace wheeled locomotion if needed, offering superior cross-country mobility
at the cost of top speed, fuel efficiency and maintenance requirements.


#### Infantry
##### Weapons
- **Bolt-action rifles:** The standard infantry firearm. Reliable, mechanically simple, magazine-fed and capable of penetrating personal armour.
- **Submachine guns:** Exist since the late WW1 period. Fire pistol-calibre ammunition, offering automatic fire at the expense of penetration and effective range.
- **Machine guns:** Both heavy and light machine guns exist. Heavy variants are primarily crew-operated, while lighter variants offer greater mobility.
- **Pistols and revolvers:** Established sidearms, generally using pistol-calibre ammunition.
- **Shotguns:** Available, primarily using conventional low-velocity ammunition.
- **Grenades:** Both fragmentation and explosive grenades exist.
- **Artillery:** Conventional field artillery, mortars and heavy artillery are established technologies.
##### Armor
- Steel armour plates (used by tanks) are available, including personal armour, shields and vehicle armour.
- Personal armour can provide protection against pistol-calibre ammunition, but rifle-calibre ammunition remains a significant threat.
#### Aviation
- Anti-aircraft magic makes combat flight impractical
#### Spacefaring
- Steam-rocket propulsion: Petroleum-fired boilers heat water, producing high-pressure steam expelled through propulsion nozzles. Water serves as reaction mass, petroleum as the energy source.
- Interplanetary travel: Steam-powered spacecraft can travel between planets, but require substantial fuel and water reserves.
- Spacecraft construction: Large, mechanically complex vessels featuring boilers, pressure vessels, pumps, turbines and radiator assemblies.
- Space infrastructure: Orbital stations, refuelling depots and industrial facilities support interplanetary operations.
- Orbital access: Spacecraft cannot independently escape planetary gravity wells. Specialized teleportation magic transports vessels between planetary surfaces and orbital spaceports.
